# 1. Executive conclusion

W2 has not found evidence that the Bonsai venture is economically validated.

W2 has, however, materially narrowed the economic problem.

## Current economic reading

### `OBSERVED`

- longitudinal recordkeeping exists;
- paid dedicated-app use exists, including multi-year persistence;
- abandonment, switching and subscription rejection also exist;
- spreadsheets, Photos, notes, Lightroom, paper and other general/analog tools are real economic substitutes;
- logging/capture burden can cause exit;
- progression/history has durable user value;
- professional teaching/organized bonsai activity exists;
- partner distribution experiments exist in the current market;
- continuity/export/data durability affect trust.

### `SUPPORTED SIGNAL`

- dedicated consumer value probably depends on **workflow mismatch**, not simply number of trees;
- record/history value alone may have a weak economic timing profile because capture cost is immediate while much of the payoff is delayed;
- consumer record-only economics may have a relatively low contribution ceiling;
- a professional payer may improve contribution per payer, but “professional” does not itself establish budget or reachable market;
- Comparable Journey is the most strategically interesting currently identified mechanism for creating recurrent non-authority value from accumulated records;
- long-horizon economics are more threatened by **revenue-duration vs service-obligation mismatch** than by raw photo-storage cost alone;
- venture-scale viability is materially more uncertain than small-business viability.

### `UNVALIDATED`

- economic eligibility prevalence;
- conversion;
- renewal;
- retention distribution;
- ARPU / LTV;
- CAC;
- market size;
- professional SaaS WTP;
- partner payment;
- provenance payment;
- Comparable Journey product/payment value;
- exact contribution margins;
- any absolute business ceiling.

## W2 answer to the governing question

> **A plausible economic structure may exist, but no current evidence supports treating the entire bonsai-growing population, dedicated tracking behavior, or observed paid persistence as sufficient proof of a business. The critical economic question is whether a materially sized segment receives enough incremental current value over tools already owned to switch, keep using and fund the duration of the service.**

The three highest-value economic falsification fronts are:

1. **dedicated consumer workflow superiority**;
2. **professional workflow + actual budget**;
3. **Comparable Journey as recurrent non-authority utility**.

No product is selected by this ordering.

# 4. Candidate monetization models

No model is selected.

## Consumer recurring
Freemium/subscription precedent exists.

**Plausibility condition:** current recurring utility and real renewal must exist.  
**Primary risk:** subscription rejection + low record-only ceiling + good-enough substitutes.

## One-time / lifetime durable utility
Market precedent exists.

**Plausibility condition:** long-term service/custody obligation remains funded or bounded.  
**Primary risk:** finite revenue against long-lived cloud/support promise.

## Hybrid durable core + recurring services
Structurally plausible.

**Plausibility condition:** users distinguish permanent record value from genuinely recurring provider services.  
**Primary risk:** complexity or artificial packaging without enough recurring value.

## Transaction / event model
Potential fit for provenance, transfer, acquisition or commerce moments.

**Plausibility condition:** sufficient event frequency × contribution.  
**Primary risk:** high value per event but ceiling too low.

## Professional SaaS / per-seat / cohort model
Plausible shape.

**Plausibility condition:** repeated professional workflow + budget + reachable payer universe.  
**Primary risk:** small market, sales/support burden, existing-tool sufficiency.

## Partner-funded / B2B2C
Plausible shape.

**Plausibility condition:** partner receives measurable commercial return.  
**Primary risk:** partner willing to distribute but unwilling to pay.

## Free utility + downstream monetization
Possible.

**Plausibility condition:** downstream value source is independently real.  
**Primary risk:** using hypothetical future marketplace/advice/partner value to subsidize an otherwise uneconomic core.

# 8. Market-size uncertainty analysis

W2 rejects:

`total bonsai population × assumed conversion`.

The economically relevant population is:

`bonsai participants`
× `job exists`
× `software willingness`
× `current substitute inadequate`
× `dedicated advantage`
× `activation/migration`
× `payment`
× `retained payment`.

Every multiplier is currently uncertain.

Therefore:

- W1 distribution is evidence that software adoption exists;
- it is not an active-user or payer census;
- competitor counts prove crowding, not TAM;
- storefront installs prove acquisition, not retention;
- ratings prove neither user count nor viability.

## G11 market-ceiling rule

**STRONG:** conservative eligible-payer case supports at least 2× the payer requirement for selected ambition.  
**MIXED:** base case works but conservative does not.  
**FAIL:** even credible upside cannot support required payers/contribution.

Failure can differ by ambition:

- fail S3 / pass S1 = plausible small business, not venture-scale;
- fail S2 / pass S1 = potentially sustainable but not founder full-time;
- fail S1 = venture-level economic warning.

A decades-long record promise creates a revenue-duration/service-duration problem.

W1 therefore moved W2 from:

> “Which tracker business model?”

to:

> **“Which segment receives enough incremental current value over substitutes to fund a durable service?”**
