# 0A. Cross-front constraints incorporated in v0.2

## F0 remains screening, not validation

W4 keeps the cheap screen:

- 0/8 target behaviors → `KILL / PARK` if instrument integrity is acceptable;
- 1/8 → `MIXED`;
- ≥2/8 independent target behaviors → `SURVIVES CHEAP FALSIFICATION`.

These thresholds show existence under favorable conditions. They do **not** estimate market prevalence, retention or economics.

## L2 F1 boundary supplied by W2

If L2 survives F0, W2 and W4 must reconcile the exact F1 protocol before execution. For a pain-qualified cohort, W2 currently defines **STRONG** as:

- **G1 Dedicated Advantage:** ≥50% continue the dedicated workflow without prompting in the observation window **and** ≥30% make a meaningful real workflow shift away from/beyond the current substitute.
- **G2 Current Value:** ≥50% experience a meaningful differentiated current-value event within 7 days **and** ≥40% return voluntarily for current utility before long-history payoff.

W4 does not invent unprovided MIXED/FAIL bands.

## L5 professional payer discovery supplied by W2

Professional workflow/budget discovery can happen before L5 Growth testing. For W2 G7B among qualified job-positive professional participants:

- ≥30% real non-zero financial commitment → STRONG;
- 10–29% → MIXED;
- <10% → FAIL.

This does not validate actor-led distribution or market conversion.

- L2 cannot be rescued by Continuity/Journey/Story if dedicated value fails.
- L3 sharing cannot be forced, prestige-driven or interpreted as success from views alone.

- L5 authority stays with the practitioner/teacher/club/institution.
- L6 cannot use prestige/pedigree/status coding.
- Continuity claims must stay within operationally supportable truth conditions.

# 1. Why falsification comes next

W1 sharpened the Growth problem.

The relevant mechanism is not simply:

> discover app → create account → retain

It is closer to:

> existing job / pain → compare against a good-enough current system → incur switching or capture cost → experience differentiated value → repeat → possibly expand or propagate

W1's working mechanism hypothesis is:

> `memory pressure + collection complexity + progression/history value + immediate utility`
>
> must exceed
>
> `capture effort + retrieval effort + subscription/business-model cost + reliability risk + lock-in risk + good-enough workaround value`.

Status: `SUPPORTED SIGNAL`, not a measured causal model.

For W4, this creates a hard ordering rule:

> **A Growth mechanism should be tested first at the link most likely to fail, before adding distribution.**

If a one-tree experience cannot produce value, buying traffic into it is merely a more expensive way to learn the same thing.

# 2. What counts as success in this artifact

None of the tests below can by itself make a loop `VALIDATED`.

The correct language is:

- **SURVIVES CHEAP FALSIFICATION** — enough behavioral evidence exists to justify a larger test;
- **MIXED / REFRAME** — part of the mechanism works, but the proposed loop does not yet close;
- **KILL / PARK** — a load-bearing link fails under a test designed to give it a fair chance;
- **DEFERRED** — the test cannot currently run because access, W2 economics or W3 permission is unresolved.

This distinction prevents an eight-person microtest from mysteriously becoming product-market fit, a recurring human hobby.

## 2.1 Screening thresholds are not market estimates

The small-sample rules below are **proposed falsification thresholds**, not prevalence estimates and not canonical truths.

They must be frozen before each test begins.

A small falsification test asks:

> **Can the target behavior be demonstrated repeatedly under conditions favorable enough that failure is informative?**

If the mechanism survives, a later confirmatory test must use a sample size and decision threshold appropriate to the economic requirement from W2 and the target segment.

## 2.2 General sequential rule

Unless a loop specifies otherwise:

### F0 · Cheap falsification

- participant mechanisms: approximately **8 eligible participants**;
- actor mechanisms: approximately **4 independent actors**;
- measure behavior rather than stated interest;
- no direct reminder for the primary endpoint;
- do not pool different segments or contexts to manufacture a pass.

### F0 interpretation

- **0 target behaviors:** `KILL / PARK`, after checking instrument integrity;
- **1 target behavior:** `MIXED`; existence is possible but weak;
- **≥2 independent target behaviors:** `SURVIVES CHEAP FALSIFICATION`, not validation.

Where a loop requires two sequential behaviors, e.g. owner shares **and** recipient activates, both links must survive independently.

### F1 · Confirmatory micro-pilot

Only after F0 survives:

- expand sample;
- pre-register a rate threshold tied to W2 economics and the segment;
- preserve a no-reminder/control condition where relevant;
- report numerator, denominator and confidence interval when estimating proportions.

F1 threshold selection is deliberately **not invented here** where the economic minimum has not yet been established.

# 3. Cross-test controls

These rules apply to L1–L5.

## 3.1 Recruitment is not Growth evidence

Recruiting a participant through a club does **not** validate clubs as distribution actors.

Recruiting through a teacher does **not** validate teacher-led acquisition.

Actor distribution is validated only when the actor independently sees enough value to repeat the mechanism with another eligible person or cohort.

## 3.2 Guided activation is not spontaneous activation

If a facilitator creates the record, asks the participant to return or reminds them to log, that behavior cannot count as the primary spontaneous-return endpoint.

Guided behavior can be recorded separately.

## 3.3 No authority-heavy advice inside the record tests

L1–L3 should not include care recommendations, diagnosis or “do X” advice.

Otherwise the test cannot distinguish:

- value from record/progression;
- from value created by unearned advice authority.

Levels 4–5 of the advice ladder remain gated by H7.

## 3.4 No forced social layer

A share option may exist in L3, but:

- participants must not be told to post publicly;
- no likes, follower counts or rankings;
- no generic feed;
- no reward for sharing.

The test is whether the object travels naturally, not whether people comply with a social-media task.

## 3.5 Instrument neutrality

Research instruments should be disposable and minimally branded.

A form, manually generated record, lightweight private page or researcher-created artifact is acceptable **as a validation instrument**, not as a product or technology decision.

## 3.6 Minimum data

Follow the Research Brief principle:

> collect the minimum needed to answer the hypothesis.

For participant artifacts, prefer metadata and participant-controlled source material over copying an entire camera roll or archive.

The most efficient order is not the numerical order of the loops.

| Priority | Test | Reason |

| **0** | L4-A · Comparable Journey corpus-density shadow | Track A; can run without participant access and clarifies whether density is structurally expensive |
| **1** | L2 · One-Tree Land-and-Expand | Directly attacks W1's strongest activation/switching constraint |
| **1** | L1 · First-Tree Inception | Minimal migration; optional workshop can produce context-specific evidence |
| **2** | L3 · Progression Artifact | Cheapest plausible test of an object actually traveling |

L1 and L2 can run in parallel if participant access exists.

# 5. L1 · First-Tree Inception

**Class:** acquisition + activation; actor repetition may later create distribution  
**Current status:** `UNVALIDATED` loop built on supported first/second-tree jobs  
**Main risk tested:** does record/progression create enough immediate value before history exists?

## 5.1 Hypothesis

> A first- or second-tree owner can get enough immediate value from a minimal record of one newly acquired tree to return voluntarily and use that record again, without needing migration or authority-heavy advice.

The test is **not** whether a beginner can be guided to create a record. That is trivial.

The test is whether the record creates a reason to return after the guided moment disappears.

## 5.2 Eligibility

Primary F0 sample:

- first or second bonsai;
- acquired within the previous seven days where possible;
- no requirement that participant already uses bonsai software;
- do not mix experienced collection managers into the primary result.

Recommended F0: **8 participants outside a workshop when feasible**.

### Optional workshop branch

The known beginner workshop may be used as an **additional context-specific branch**.

Its result must be labeled:

> `WORKSHOP-CONTEXT SIGNAL`

and cannot rescue failure in an independent acquisition context.

The canon already warns that workshop activation is artificially high.

## 5.3 Smallest instrument

A disposable **minimal tree record** containing only:

- one tree/photo;
- acquisition date or approximate date;
- species if known, with uncertainty allowed;
- one short “starting point” note;
- subsequent event/photo/note capability.

No care guide.  
No recommendations.  
No community.  
No provenance ceremony.  
No full collection import.

The technical implementation may be a simple private form + generated timeline or equivalent research instrument.

## 5.4 Procedure

### Day 0

1. Participant creates the minimal baseline.
2. Researcher records completion time and points of confusion.
3. Participant is told only that the record remains available if they find it useful.
4. Do **not** instruct them to make a second entry by a deadline.

### Days 1–30

Primary condition:

- no direct reminder to log;
- passive observation of meaningful actions where technically possible.

A meaningful repeat action is:

- add a real event;
- add a new photo/update;
- add/correct a factual record;
- deliberately return to retrieve prior information.

A notification-open or accidental page view does not count.

### Day 30 research contact

The day-30 interview may ask:

- whether the record was used;
- what caused the return;
- what the participant used instead when they did not return;
- whether the value was memory/progression/organization or care guidance.

The day-30 prompted interaction **does not count** as spontaneous return.

## 5.5 Primary evidence

- `meaningful_spontaneous_return_within_30d`
- `reason_for_return`
- `alternative_used_when_no_return`

Secondary:

- baseline creation effort;
- first event chosen voluntarily;
- whether progression/photo value appears before a long history exists;
- difference between workshop-guided and independent context.

## 5.6 F0 decision rule

With 8 eligible participants:

- **≥2 spontaneous meaningful returns:** `SURVIVES CHEAP FALSIFICATION`;
- **1:** `MIXED`; investigate whether the job exists but value timing is too slow;
- **0:** `KILL / PARK L1 as a record-led first-tree mechanism`, provided participants had continued access and the instrument functioned.

A workshop-only pass cannot promote L1 beyond `CONTEXT-SPECIFIC SIGNAL`.

## 5.7 What would not count as a pass

- 8/8 create a record while an instructor watches;
- users return because the researcher sends “please add an update”;
- users return only to receive care advice;
- users say the concept is useful but never reopen it.

## 5.8 Evidence produced

If L1 survives, it informs:

- immediate value before historical accumulation;
- whether first-tree onboarding can avoid migration entirely;
- first events people naturally consider worth remembering;
- whether record/progression can activate without an advisor;
- whether an actor-led acquisition test is worth attempting next.

## 5.9 Non-generalization boundary

This does **not** validate:

- experienced growers;
- collection-scale migration;
- nursery distribution;
- workshop distribution generally;
- long-term retention;
- WTP;
- network effects.

# 6. L2 · One-Tree Land-and-Expand

**Class:** activation / expansion mechanism, not user-to-user acquisition  
**Current status:** `UNVALIDATED`  
**Main risk tested:** can differentiated value appear before bulk migration?

This is the most direct falsification of W1's sharpened Growth question.

## 6.1 Hypothesis

> An expanding grower who already has a good-enough recording system can experience enough incremental value from one tree to voluntarily move/add another tree before being asked to migrate the collection.

The load-bearing behavior is **voluntary expansion after one-tree value**.

## 6.2 Eligibility

Primary segment:

- approximately 5+ trees;
- currently uses at least one system such as Photos, spreadsheet, notes, paper or a dedicated tracker;
- has enough existing history for one selected tree to support a real retrieval/progression task.

Recommended F0: **8 participants**.

Do not combine first-tree owners with this result.

## 6.3 Two-stage instrument

L2 deliberately separates **value ceiling** from **capture burden**.

### Stage A · Concierge one-tree conversion

Participant selects one tree that matters to them.

Researcher helps turn only that tree's existing material into a compact chronological record.

The researcher may do most of the assembly so that the first question is:

> **Is the resulting object meaningfully better than the participant's current workaround when migration effort is temporarily removed?**

If the answer is no even under concierge conditions, there is no reason to test bulk migration.

### Stage B · Burden restoration

After the one-tree record is delivered:

- participant receives the same mechanism to add another tree;
- no request to migrate the collection;
- no completion target;
- researcher does not assemble the second tree for the primary endpoint.

## 6.4 Pre/post behavioral task

Before Stage A, ask participant to perform one real retrieval task using their current system, e.g.:

- find the earliest photo of the selected tree;
- find/estimate the last repot or intervention;
- show progression between two periods;
- reconstruct a short sequence of changes.

Record:

- time;
- uncertainty;
- number of tools/places searched;
- participant's own description of effort.

Repeat an equivalent task with the one-tree record after assembly.

This is not a fair product benchmark. It is a **value-ceiling probe**: if even a curated record creates no meaningful retrieval/progression advantage, the dedicated-system thesis is weaker.

## 6.5 Primary behavior

Within seven days of receiving the one-tree record, without a migration prompt:

`voluntary_second_tree_addition_or_request`

Stronger version:

`participant_self_adds_second_tree`

Also record whether the participant returns to the original workaround instead.

## 6.6 F0 decision rule

With 8 eligible participants:

### Value ceiling gate

- if **0–1** participants find a concrete retrieval/progression advantage even after concierge assembly: `KILL / PARK L2` and weaken the dedicated-record thesis for this segment;
- if **≥2** show concrete advantage: proceed to expansion gate.

### Expansion gate

- **≥2 independent self-additions / explicit unprompted requests to add another tree:** `SURVIVES CHEAP FALSIFICATION`;
- **1:** `MIXED`;
- **0:** `KILL L2 as land-and-expand`, even if the one-tree object itself is liked.

A participant saying “I would add the rest later” without doing so does not count.

## 6.7 Critical diagnostic outcomes

L2 can fail in several informative ways:

### A. No incremental value

Photos/Excel/notes remain sufficient.

Implication:

> dedicated record may not create enough switching value for this segment.

### B. Value exists, expansion does not

Implication:

> one-tree experience may be useful, but collection migration/capture burden blocks expansion.

### C. Expansion occurs only with concierge help

Implication:

> the value proposition may survive, but the operational/product burden is unresolved.

### D. Expansion happens voluntarily

Implication:

> L2 survives to a larger migration-retention test; it does not yet prove sustainable logging.

## 6.8 Evidence produced

- incremental value versus good-enough workarounds;
- time-to-value;
- migration avoidance;
- whether value from one tree causes expansion;
- which current systems are hardest to displace;
- what history users choose to migrate first.

## 6.9 Non-generalization boundary

Does not validate:

- acquisition of people who do not already record;
- full-collection completion;
- long-term logging retention;
- WTP;
- user-to-user Growth.

# 7. L3 · Progression Artifact

**Class:** object-led propagation candidate  
**Current status:** progression/photo value `OBSERVED`; product-level travel and recipient activation `UNVALIDATED`  
**Main risk tested:** can a valuable private artifact actually travel and cause another meaningful action?

## 7.1 Hypothesis

> A coherent progression artifact created from a grower's real tree history is valuable enough that some owners voluntarily share it, and some recipients take a meaningful next action rather than merely viewing it.

L3 has two separate gates:

1. **object travel**;
2. **recipient activation**.

A pretty artifact that gets views is not yet a Growth loop.

## 7.2 Eligibility

Owners:

- real tree with meaningful photo/history span;
- preferably ≥6 months of material;
- enough material to show change over time;
- no requirement that they currently use dedicated bonsai software.

Recommended F0:

- **8 owners**;
- recipient behaviors tracked separately and not pooled as owner observations.

## 7.3 Smallest instrument

Researcher manually creates a neutral shareable progression artifact from participant-provided material.

Minimum content:

- tree identity/name as the owner uses it;
- chronological photos;
- a few dated events or notes where available;
- no score, ranking, diagnosis or care recommendation.

The owner is told the artifact may remain private or may be shared using the available link.

Do **not** instruct them to publish it.

## 7.4 Primary behaviors

### Gate A · Owner travel

Within seven days:

`owner_voluntarily_shares_artifact`

Sharing can be private or public. Channel is irrelevant to this test.

### Gate B · Recipient activation

A recipient meaningful action may include:

- requests/follows a future update on that specific tree;
- saves the progression for later learning in a way observable by the instrument;
- asks to create/reconstruct a progression for their own tree;
- submits their own comparable tree/history through an optional action;
- meaningfully contacts the owner about the tree/journey.

A click, like or passive view alone does not count.

## 7.5 F0 decision rule

### Travel gate

With 8 owners:

- **≥2 voluntarily share:** object travel survives cheap falsification;
- **1:** mixed;
- **0:** kill L3 as a natural traveling-object mechanism.

### Recipient gate

Across shared artifacts:

- **meaningful recipient action associated with at least 2 independent owner artifacts:** `SURVIVES CHEAP FALSIFICATION as a propagation candidate`;
- meaningful actions from only one artifact: `MIXED`, possible creator-specific effect;
- shares occur but no meaningful recipient action: **retain progression as owner value; kill/park L3 as a Growth loop**.

## 7.6 Important confounds

- artifact novelty;
- researcher curation quality;
- unusually dramatic trees/progressions;
- owner already being a creator with an audience;
- recipient friendship/social obligation;
- visual polish creating entertainment value unrelated to record utility.

Record these rather than averaging them away.

## 7.7 Stronger follow-up if F0 survives

Create paired artifacts varying only one dimension:

- story-rich versus bare before/after;
- context-rich versus visually polished;
- short versus long chronology.

Measure which version produces meaningful recipient action.

This becomes a W3 dependency because framing/story may influence travel.

## 7.8 Evidence produced

- whether progression is merely private retention value or a traveling object;
- which artifact components motivate sharing;
- whether recipients want the tree, the learning, the story or the tool;
- whether object travel can produce a creator/contributor transition.

## 7.9 Non-generalization boundary

Does not validate:

- social feed;
- virality coefficient;
- generic creator marketing;
- public community retention;
- conversion economics.

# 8. L4 · Comparable Journey Contribution

**Class:** discovery + evidence-producing contribution candidate  
**Current status:** public progression as learning infrastructure `OBSERVED`; Comparable Journey product value H6B `UNVALIDATED`  
**Main risks tested:** corpus density, comparability and contribution after value.

L4 is split into a **Track A feasibility test** and a **Track B utility/contribution test**.

---

## 8A. L4-A · Corpus-Density Shadow Test

**Track:** A · zero-participant  
**Runnable in principle now**, subject to permitted source access  
**Does not establish user value**

### 8A.1 Hypothesis

> Public documented bonsai journeys contain enough contextual structure that useful-looking comparison sets can be formed without requiring absurd network density.

This is a structural feasibility question, not a demand test.

### 8A.2 Smallest instrument

Use **20–30 real public progression histories** initially, consistent with the Research Brief's shadow-test logic.

Represent each case with only fields observable or explicitly stated:

- species / identification confidence;
- broad climate/location where stated;
- stage/state where stated;
- intervention;
- intent where stated;
- observation;
- outcome;
- time relation;
- evidence availability.

Never infer missing fields silently.

### 8A.3 Create query cases

From the same source set or other permitted public material, create approximately **10–15 real query shapes**, such as:

`species + recent repot + yellowing + climate known`

or

`same species + first structural prune + early development`.

Do not invent a recommendation.

### 8A.4 Match tiers

For every query, count candidate matches at three levels:

**Tier 1 · strict**  
`species × climate × stage/state × intervention`

**Tier 2 · useful-relaxed**  
`species × intervention × relevant state/context`

**Tier 3 · broad**  
`adjacent species/context or same intervention without enough state similarity`

The point is to identify where the corpus fragments.

### 8A.5 Primary outputs

- number of candidate matches per query at each tier;
- missing-context rate for each matching dimension;
- which dimensions destroy density most quickly;
- whether outcomes can be compared descriptively without conflating cause;
- how many apparently similar cases become non-comparable once context is considered.

### 8A.6 Decision interpretation

Because 20–30 cases cannot estimate real network density:

- **several query shapes produce plausible Tier 1/2 matches:** L4-A `SURVIVES STRUCTURAL SHADOW TEST`;
- **matches exist only after stripping most meaningful context:** `MIXED / DENSITY RISK HIGH`;
- **records are too context-poor to construct defensible comparisons at all:** `PARK L4` until a different data source or capture model exists.

Do **not** convert counts from this convenience set into prevalence or a user-number threshold.

### 8A.7 Evidence produced

- minimum useful context;
- likely cold-start density cost;
- fields that matter to comparability;
- whether manual matching is plausible before network scale;
- whether L4 should reach participant testing at all.

---

## 8B. L4-B · Curated Comparable Journey Utility + Contribution

**Track:** B · participant  
**Status:** `UNVALIDATED`, blocked until suitable participant access  
**Run only if L4-A survives**

### 8B.1 Hypothesis

> A grower with a real question can derive useful learning from a small set of structurally comparable journeys without receiving a recommendation, and some users will contribute their own case after receiving that value.

This contains two different claims:

1. Comparable Journey has standalone utility;
2. utility can feed evidence production.

The second may fail while the first survives.

### 8B.2 Eligibility

Approximately **8 growers** with a current or recent real question involving:

- a technique;
- recovery/failure;
- progression decision;
- new species/context;
- timing or state uncertainty.

Do not recruit only people already enthusiastic about tracking apps.

### 8B.3 Smallest instrument

Researcher manually curates **2–3 public comparable journeys** for each participant.

The packet contains:

- why each case is considered comparable;
- observed context;
- what happened;
- uncertainty/missing fields;
- no recommendation;
- no “best answer”.

### 8B.4 Primary utility endpoint

The Research Brief's H6B criterion remains relevant:

> a comparable case is reported as useful when it carries no recommendation.

Behavioral support should also be captured where possible:

- participant chooses to inspect/save one case;
- uses it to refine what they would search/ask next;
- identifies a comparison dimension they consider important;
- returns to another comparable case later.

### 8B.5 Contribution endpoint

After the comparison experience, expose a **neutral optional mechanism** to add the participant's own case to the research corpus.

Do not reward contribution.

Count:

`participant_voluntarily_contributes_case`

The contribution can be minimal and should not require full structured logging.

### 8B.6 F0 decision rule

With 8 eligible participants:

#### Utility

- **≥2 demonstrate/report concrete usefulness without a recommendation:** L4 utility survives cheap falsification;
- 1: mixed;
- 0: kill/park Comparable Journey as a user-value proposition.

#### Evidence-producing loop

- **≥2 voluntarily contribute their own case after receiving value:** contribution mechanism survives cheap falsification;
- 1: mixed;
- 0: **retain Comparable Journey as retrieval value if utility passed, but kill/park the self-reinforcing contribution loop**.

### 8B.7 Density degradation test

If utility survives, a later test should intentionally vary match quality:

- close match;
- partial match;
- broad/weak match.

The goal is to find the **minimum comparability users still consider useful**, because that determines the real network-density requirement.

### 8B.8 Evidence produced

- H6B;
- dimensions users actually use to judge comparability;
- minimum match quality;
- whether consumption leads to contribution;
- whether contribution requires excessive structured capture;
- likely user-1 / user-100 / user-1,000 behavior without pretending those numbers are validated thresholds.

# 9. L5 · Teacher / Club Continuity

**Class:** actor-led distribution + recurring continuity + possible attestation  
**Current status:** recurring small-group learning `OBSERVED`; teacher/club distribution for this venture `UNVALIDATED`  
**Main risks tested:** actor value, repetition and administrative burden.

## 9.1 Hypothesis

> A teacher or recurring bonsai group receives enough continuity value from a lightweight record across sessions that the actor independently chooses to reuse the mechanism, without becoming an unpaid data-entry operator.

Actor repetition is the load-bearing Growth behavior.

One successful workshop session is not a loop.

## 9.2 Eligibility

Recommended F0:

- approximately **4 independent teachers, practitioners or recurring-group organizers** where feasible;
- each must have a real repeated learning context, not a one-off hypothetical;
- avoid treating four instructors from the same organization as four independent distribution systems.

If only one actor is accessible, label evidence as a **case study**, not actor validation.

## 9.3 Pre-test burden ceiling

Before showing the instrument, ask each actor to state:

- what continuity problem exists between sessions, if any;
- what maximum additional work per student/tree/session they would tolerate;
- who they believe should capture the event: teacher, student, assistant or nobody.

This creates an actor-specific burden threshold **before** seeing the mechanism and avoids inventing a universal “two minutes is fine” rule afterward.

## 9.4 Two-stage concierge design

### Stage A · Value ceiling with zero actor administration

For a small real set of approximately 3–5 student trees per actor:

- researcher captures the minimum session record;
- researcher prepares a continuity pack before the next session;
- actor incurs almost no logging burden.

At the next real session, observe whether the actor actually consults/uses the record.

Primary Stage A question:

> **Does continuity create operational/teaching value even when capture burden is removed?**

If not, there is no reason to optimize capture.

### Stage B · Burden restoration

If Stage A creates value:

- one subsequent event is captured by the person the actor said should own the task;
- measure actual time/friction against the actor's predeclared burden ceiling;
- researcher stops doing the primary capture.

## 9.5 Actor repetition endpoint

After experiencing at least one real continuity cycle, measure whether the actor independently:

- asks to use it again next session;
- applies it to another student/tree;
- proposes using it with another cohort;
- incorporates it into a repeated workflow.

A polite “interesting idea” does not count.

## 9.6 F0 decision rule

Across approximately 4 independent actors:

### Continuity-value gate

- **≥2 actually use the concierge continuity pack in a subsequent real session:** value survives cheap falsification;
- 1: mixed;
- 0: kill/park L5.

### Repetition + burden gate

For L5 to survive as actor-led distribution:

- at least **2 independent actors** choose to repeat/extend the mechanism;
- and their real capture workflow does not exceed their own predeclared burden ceiling.

If continuity value exists only when the researcher does all capture:

> `MIXED · CONCIERGE VALUE / DISTRIBUTION LOOP NOT YET VIABLE`.

If actors repeat but W2 later shows acquisition/support economics impossible:

> behavior survives; sustainable Growth loop fails economic reconciliation.

## 9.7 Optional attestation subtest

Do not make H5 a requirement for L5.

But if a teacher/practitioner has performed or observed a known event, test a minimal attestation:

`Confirm` · `Correct` · `Cannot verify`

Record:

- willingness to attach their name/role to the specific claim;
- friction;
- corrections;
- refusal reasons.

This creates useful H5 evidence without turning the entire teacher test into a provenance experiment.

## 9.8 Evidence produced

- teacher/club continuity value;
- actual versus tolerated administrative burden;
- who should capture events;
- actor repetition;
- early P3 distribution logic;
- optional H5 attestation feasibility;
- whether a recurring actor can generate both users and evidence.

## 9.9 Non-generalization boundary

Does not validate:

- nursery distribution;
- global teacher market;
- teacher WTP;
- sales cycle;
- SaaS economics;
- community/network retention outside the cohort.

Those require W2 and broader validation.

# 13. W2 dependencies to freeze before F1

F0 can run without knowing final economics. F1 cannot be interpreted as a sustainable Growth mechanism until W2 provides boundaries for:

1. acceptable acquisition/servicing cost per user;
2. consumer WTP or viable value-capture range;
3. whether one-time, subscription or other models materially alter acquisition resistance;
4. cost of long-term storage/export/continuity obligations;
5. teacher/club economics for L5;
6. nursery/retailer incentive if L1 later tests purchase-origin distribution;
7. market ceiling and what scale would count as success.

### Why this matters

If only 5% of an actor's participants activate but the actor acquisition and support cost is near zero, that may be viable.

If a 40% activation rate requires founder-led sales and manual servicing for a €3/month product, it may be economically useless.

Therefore F1 rate thresholds should be tied to W2, not invented by W4 in isolation.
