# Bonsai · W1 · Exit & Persistence Review v0.4

**Date:** 3 September 2026  
**Status:** PRE-VALIDATION / Track A  
**Corpus:** 82 derived evidence units total; this memo intentionally oversamples explicit exit, avoidance, switching and persistence behavior.  
**Important:** because this is an enriched sample, **no proportion in this memo is a market-prevalence estimate**.

---

## 1. Why this round matters

Earlier W1 rounds established that users can value bonsai tracking, photo history,
progression and reminders.

That was necessary but insufficient.

The load-bearing question is not whether some people like the idea. It is:

> **Does the value survive repeated capture cost, reliability failures,
> subscription resistance and good-enough alternatives?**

This round searched specifically for behavior with consequences:
- uninstall,
- switching,
- abandoning a tracking method,
- reverting to general tools,
- refusing adoption,
- continued daily use,
- continued paid use,
- and anxiety about losing an accumulated record.

That produces much stronger evidence than feature requests.

---

## 2. Direct exit behavior now exists

### A. Same-day uninstall and fallback to general tools
A Bonsai Care App user reports:
1. installing the app,
2. encountering unskippable watering notifications, limited useful guidance and
   paywall friction,
3. uninstalling the next morning,
4. explicitly returning to Google, a text document and phone alarms.

**Status: `OBSERVED`**

This is the clearest counterexample yet to:
> “A specialized bonsai app automatically beats general tools.”

It does not.

### B. Direct competitor switch
An Appy Bonsai reviewer says photo-selection limitations caused them to move to
YAMA BONSAI.

**Status: `OBSERVED`**

Here the job survived; the implementation lost.

This is evidence for:
> `implementation failure → switching`

not:
> `tracking job failure`.

### C. Developer-acknowledged churn
In July 2026 Appy Bonsai explicitly invited users who had stopped using the app
because of bugs/stability issues to return after a stabilization release.

**Status: `OBSERVED` that the developer acknowledged churn for this cause.**

This does not provide a churn rate or denominator.

---

## 3. The strongest falsification pressure: dedicated apps can lose to generic tools

Direct public behavior now includes:

- Bonsai Care App → Google + text document + phone alarms;
- Mirai My Trees → Excel + photo folders because they are easier;
- structured app + spreadsheet → abandoned in favor of Lightroom photos,
  metadata and keywords;
- multiple bonsai apps tried → no more useful than applications already on the
  phone;
- Bonsai Droid → switched to desktop bonsai software because updating was easier;
- spreadsheet + printed paper calendar → durable hybrid system.

### Finding

**`OBSERVED`: general-purpose and analog systems are real competitors for the
record job.**

This is no longer merely a strategic inference.

---

## 4. But the opposite behavior is also observed

W1 must not cherry-pick churn.

Counter-evidence includes:

- Bonsai Album user reporting **daily use**;
- Bonsai Care App user reporting **paid use since launch**;
- BonsaiDo reviewer reporting **more than two years of paid use** before a data
  failure;
- experienced growers describing detailed records as highly valuable;
- a Bonsai Care App user worried that insufficient app uptake could force them
  to find a new tracking system.

### Finding

**`OBSERVED`: durable and paid longitudinal use exists for at least some users.**

Therefore:

> `Nobody sustains bonsai tracking` is **CONTRADICTED** by observed cases.

But:

> `Enough growers sustain it to support a venture` remains **UNVALIDATED**.

## 5. The emerging behavioral model

The evidence now supports a sharper model:

### Adoption happens when
`memory/problem pressure + collection complexity + visible progression value`
is greater than the user's current workaround cost.

### Continued use happens when
`retrieval/history/reminder value`
continues to exceed
`capture friction + maintenance + subscription + reliability + lock-in risk`.

### Exit happens through at least four observed mechanisms

#### 1. Implementation failure
- bugs,
- photo handling,
- sync,
- notification behavior,
- cumbersome input.

#### 2. No incremental value
The user concludes that notes, alarms, Photos, Excel or folders already do
enough.

#### 3. Business-model rejection
Some users explicitly reject subscriptions regardless of utility or demand a
clear advantage before switching from a free spreadsheet.

#### 4. Durability / trust failure
A decades-long hobby creates a mismatch with software that may disappear,
lose data or trap records.

This model is a **SUPPORTED SIGNAL**, not a causal estimate.

---

## 6. A more precise statement about logging friction

Earlier W1 said implementation friction was more visible than rejection of
logging itself.

That remains true, but v0.4 adds a correction:

> **The job can also be rejected indirectly when the user decides that the
> incremental value of specialized logging does not justify switching from a
> simpler general-purpose system.**

So there are now three analytically distinct outcomes:

1. **JOB VALUE + APP VALUE**  
   User tracks and keeps using the dedicated app.

2. **JOB VALUE + APP FAILURE**  
   User still tracks, but switches app or returns to spreadsheet/photos/notes.

3. **LOW JOB VALUE / LOW RECORD INTENSITY**  
   User takes photos occasionally, relies on memory, or does not systematically
   record.

Reviews are good at #1 and #2.
They remain poor at estimating the prevalence of #3.

## 7. The “decades problem”

One Bonsai Nut user makes the strategic risk unusually explicit:

- bonsai are maintained for decades;
- an app would therefore need to survive for decades;
- they do not trust a random solo-developer app to do so;
- loss of the service could mean loss of accumulated photos/history;
- they would refuse the app even if free.

This is one person's behavior/stance, not prevalence.

But it exposes a structurally important tension:

> **The longer the product succeeds at becoming a system of record, the more
> consequential product mortality becomes.**

That links:
- retention,
- export,
- data ownership,
- trust,
- monetization,
- and company durability.

It is not a blockchain argument.
It is a continuity argument.

---

## 8. Subscription resistance is behavioral, not hypothetical

Observed public responses include:
- strong dislike of SaaS/subscriptions;
- preference for one-time purchase;
- explicit question: why switch from a free spreadsheet?
- user saying existing apps offer little beyond built-in phone tools;
- Google Play criticism that required functionality sits behind subscription.

Counter-evidence:
- explicit annual conversion after trial;
- paid use since launch;
- paid use for >2 years;
- two paid competing apps used simultaneously by an experienced grower.

### Correct status

- `OBSERVED`: both subscription acceptance and subscription rejection exist.
- `UNVALIDATED`: distribution of either behavior.
- `UNVALIDATED`: optimal pricing model.

W2 therefore must not model consumer willingness to pay as a single average.

## 9. Segment implications strengthened

### Beginner / low experience
More likely in current evidence to value:
- care information,
- reminders,
- reassurance,
- simple capture.

Risk:
- can churn immediately if reminders/guidance feel wrong or paywall appears
  before distinctive value.

### Expanding collection
Value shifts toward:
- organization,
- retrieval,
- batching,
- photos,
- avoiding memory overload.

### Experienced / long horizon
Evidence splits:
- some strongly value detailed records;
- some prefer Excel/photos/custom workflows;
- generic reminders/advice lose credibility;
- software longevity and export matter more.

### Non-software / low-screen-time
This is now an observable segment, not noise:
- notebook,
- pencil,
- albums,
- photo metadata,
- memory.

A dedicated app may never be the right product for them.

## 10. What this does to P1

P1 remains `UNVALIDATED`.

### Evidence supporting P1
- repeated value attached to history/progression;
- direct daily and multi-year use;
- paid continued use exists;
- record loss creates strong emotional/functional cost;
- collection growth increases memory pressure.

### Evidence against / pressure on P1
- general tools can be good enough;
- users abandon dedicated tracking for Photos/Excel/metadata;
- subscription can block adoption;
- long-term trust is difficult;
- every extra field adds recurring cost;
- a decades-long record magnifies platform-survival risk.

### W1 verdict at this stage
Not:
> `P1 passes`

Not:
> `P1 fails`

But:
> **The record behavior exists; the venture-level question has shifted from
> existence to prevalence, persistence and incremental advantage over
> general-purpose tools.**

That is a materially sharper uncertainty.

## 12. W2 handoff

New economics questions:

1. Is subscription structurally mismatched with a hobby where records need
   decades-long continuity?
2. Is lifetime / one-time purchase economically possible given ongoing storage
   and support?
3. Can export/data portability reduce subscription resistance?
4. Which segment has enough collection complexity to perceive paid incremental
   value?
5. Does free-tier tree count create conversion or simply trigger workaround
   reversion?

No answer yet.

## 13. W3 handoff

Potential credible territories gain evidence:
- memory,
- continuity,
- progress,
- ownership of one's history,
- calm organization.

Potentially dangerous promises:
- “we are your permanent record” without credible continuity/export;
- high-authority care/AI claims;
- over-structured “professional” language for beginners.

Trust may be earned partly by:
> **making it easy to leave with your data.**

That is an implication, not yet a brand recommendation.

## 14. W4 handoff

New acquisition moments:
- spreadsheet becomes annoying;
- collection size crosses memory threshold;
- photo history becomes fragmented;
- existing tracker fails;
- user fears product shutdown/data lock-in;
- migration from another app.

Possible distribution object:
- progression history / before-after export.

Growth warning:
If activation requires importing 30 trees before the value becomes visible,
the switching cost may kill the loop before it starts.

## 15. What W1 still cannot answer from reviews

The next unresolved questions are now very specific:

1. Among growers who intentionally record trees, what share use an app vs
   spreadsheet/photos/notebook?
2. What share sustain the behavior for 6+ months?
3. At what collection size does structured tracking become worth its cost?
4. Which fields are actually recorded repeatedly versus filled once and ignored?
5. Does batch logging materially improve persistence?
6. Are reminders a retention engine or a notification-compliance illusion?
7. Does export increase willingness to commit a full collection?
8. What causes users to stop recording entirely, rather than switch tools?
9. How much of observed paid use survives beyond the first renewal?
10. Which segment is large enough to support the venture?

These require either broader behavioral evidence or Track B.

---

## 16. Current W1 conclusion

The strongest defensible statement after this exit-focused round is:

> **Bonsai growers demonstrably maintain longitudinal records in multiple forms,
> and some sustain dedicated-app use for years. But dedicated bonsai software
> competes against highly flexible, cheap and durable general-purpose tools.
> Churn can result not only from bugs, but from failure to create enough
> incremental value to justify repeated capture, subscription and long-term
> trust.**

Evidence status:
**SUPPORTED SIGNAL**, backed by multiple `OBSERVED` behaviors.

The central falsification question for P1 is now:

> **Is there a sufficiently large segment for whom a dedicated per-tree record
> creates recurring value that general-purpose tools cannot deliver with lower
> friction?**

That question is still open.
