## 0. Read this first

W1 does **not** conclude that Bonsai should build a tracker.

W1 concludes that the behavioral problem is more specific than the original
market scan suggested:

> **Some growers sustain longitudinal records for months/years and derive value
> from history, progression, reminders and collection memory. Dedicated bonsai
> software loses when incremental value fails to exceed repeated capture cost,
> organization/retrieval burden, subscription resistance, reliability/continuity
> risk and the convenience of general-purpose tools.**

Evidence status: `SUPPORTED SIGNAL`, built from multiple `OBSERVED` behaviors.

# W2 · Monetization & Economics

## Evidence W2 may treat as OBSERVED

- Some users pay for bonsai tracking/care software.
- Multi-year paid use exists in at least one directly observed case.
- Users also explicitly reject subscriptions/SaaS.
- Users compare paid products against free spreadsheets/photos/notes.
- Some users use two paid bonsai apps simultaneously.
- Dedicated software is not the only substitute; Excel, Photos, OneNote,
  Evernote, notebooks and hybrid systems are real alternatives.
- Data continuity/export concern appears before purchase or migration.
- At least three known Android products are in the 10K+ Google Play bracket.

## SUPPORTED SIGNALS relevant to W2

- Willingness to pay may rise with collection complexity and record dependence.
- Subscription economics may conflict with a decades-long continuity expectation.
- Export/data portability may reduce purchase and lock-in resistance.
- A tree-count paywall may convert some users and send others back to free tools.

## Still UNVALIDATED

- conversion rate;
- renewal rate;
- churn;
- ARPU/LTV;
- CAC;
- viable market ceiling;
- optimal pricing model;
- whether 5,000 paying users is attainable or economically sufficient.

## Sharpened W2 question

> **What recurring value is strong enough to justify payment when the user can
> already solve much of the job with tools they own?**

# W3 · Brand Identity & Marketing

## User language W1 repeatedly observes

- remember;
- history;
- progression;
- photos;
- what I did;
- when I did it;
- my trees;
- one place;
- keep track;
- don’t lose it;
- not too complicated;
- my rules / my experience.

## Supported territory

Potentially credible:
- memory;
- continuity;
- progression;
- calm organization;
- ownership of one's history;
- confidence from recall rather than authority.

Potentially risky:
- expert replacement;
- generic AI authority;
- “permanent record” without credible continuity/export;
- professional/database language that raises capture burden.

## Important contradiction

Users do not uniformly want more structure.

Some actively prefer:
- photos,
- notebooks,
- spreadsheets,
- minimal notes.

Therefore “comprehensive” is not automatically positive brand value.

## Sharpened W3 question

> **What meaning can Bonsai credibly own that makes the record feel valuable,
> not bureaucratic, while avoiding expertise claims it cannot yet justify?**

# W4 · Growth Strategy

## OBSERVED acquisition moments

- first tree / hobby feels intimidating;
- collection outgrows memory;
- spreadsheet becomes annoying;
- photo history becomes fragmented;
- user forgets previous work;
- existing tracker fails;
- user wants migration/export security;
- desire to show progression;
- purchase/identification uncertainty.

## OBSERVED traveling objects

- before/after;
- progression photos;
- tree history;
- shareable/exported record.

## Growth constraints

- asking users to recreate 20–50 trees before value is visible creates
  switching friction;
- reminder overload can destroy activation/retention;
- subscription can block switching from a free workaround;
- a dead or unstable product creates distrust precisely because the history is
  long-lived.

## Supported loops worth investigating

- `capture → visible progression → share → new user sees value → starts own record`
- `current tool failure → import/migrate → immediate restored history value`
- `first tree → lightweight record → progression becomes more valuable with time`

None is validated as a growth loop yet.

## Sharpened W4 question

> **Which acquisition moment lets the user experience value before asking them
> to invest heavily in rebuilding their history?**

# Shared contradictions all three fronts must preserve

1. Durable app use **does exist**.
2. App abandonment **also exists**.
3. Paid use **does exist**.
4. Subscription rejection **also exists**.
5. Experienced growers **can value detailed records**.
6. Experienced growers **can also prefer spreadsheets/photos/custom tools**.
7. Reminders **can be useful**.
8. Reminders **can also be noisy or horticulturally misleading**.
9. More structure **can improve retrieval**.
10. More structure **can make capture intolerable**.

Do not average these into a fictional “average bonsai user”.
