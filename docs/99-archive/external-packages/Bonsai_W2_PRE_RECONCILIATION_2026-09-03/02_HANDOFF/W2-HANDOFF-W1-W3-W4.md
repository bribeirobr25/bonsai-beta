## 2. W2's current economic reading

### `OBSERVED`

- longitudinal recordkeeping exists;
- paid use and multi-year paid persistence exist;
- abandonment, switching and subscription rejection also exist;
- general-purpose and analog tools are real economic substitutes;
- logging/data-entry burden can cause exit;
- progression/history carries durable value;
- continuity/export/service mortality affect trust;
- professional teaching and partner-distribution surfaces exist.

### `SUPPORTED SIGNAL`

- dedicated consumer value appears more likely to depend on workflow mismatch than
  on tree count alone;
- a viable record proposition probably needs meaningful current utility before
  long-horizon history becomes valuable;
- consumer record-only contribution may have a relatively low ceiling;
- professional payers may have higher contribution potential, but workflow, budget
  and reachable payer count are all unvalidated;
- Comparable Journey is currently the strongest identified non-authority mechanism
  for converting accumulated records into recurring present utility;
- venture-scale viability is more uncertain than small-business viability.

### `UNVALIDATED`

- economic eligibility prevalence;
- conversion and renewal;
- retention distribution;
- ARPU/LTV;
- CAC;
- absolute market size;
- professional SaaS willingness to pay;
- partner payment;
- provenance monetization;
- Comparable Journey payment impact;
- exact unit economics.

# 5. Requests to W3 · Brand Identity & Marketing

W2 asks W3 to return explicit answers to the following before Cross-Front Reconciliation.

## W3-Q1 · Immediate value versus accumulated meaning

W2 sees a potential split:

- immediate economic value: organization, retrieval, memory, current workflow;
- accumulated/emotional value: progression, history, continuity, story.

**Question:**  
Does W3 find that the most credible brand territory can hold both without making the
economic proposition sound like bureaucracy or reducing the brand to a utility?

## W3-Q2 · Ownership versus recurring monetization

W1/W2 show continuity, export and service mortality affect trust.

**Question:**  
Does W3 conclude that users must perceive the tree record/history as fundamentally
“theirs”? If yes, what brand/trust constraints should W2 treat as non-negotiable when
later evaluating recurring payment?

W2 specifically wants to avoid a model that feels like:

> “pay forever or lose access to your own history.”

## W3-Q3 · Authority boundary

W2 has deliberately deferred authority-dependent Decision Intelligence.

**Question:**  
Which claims can W3 credibly make around memory, progress, learning, confidence and
continuity **without** implying practitioner authority or horticultural certainty?

## W3-Q4 · Segment tension

W2 may discover that:

- the person emotionally attracted to history/story;
- the person who gets immediate workflow value;
- and the payer

are not always the same profile.

**Question:**  
Does W3's segmentation/value territory create any tension with W2's current recipient
× payer map that should be surfaced before product selection?

## W3-Q5 · Comparable Journey meaning

**Question:**  
If Comparable Journey survives validation, does W3 see it primarily as:
- learning,
- belonging,
- progress,
- reassurance,
- evidence,
- or another territory?

W2 needs this because the economic value of the mechanic may depend on the meaning
users assign to it.

# 6. Requests to W4 · Growth Strategy

W2 asks W4 to return explicit answers to the following before reconciliation.

## W4-Q1 · Distribution actor versus payer

W2 now distinguishes these rigorously.

A nursery, workshop, club or teacher can distribute without paying.

**Question:**  
For each actor W4 considers, classify separately:
- distribution incentive;
- recipient value;
- payer incentive;
- evidence produced by the loop.

Do not convert partnership access into partner revenue.

## W4-Q2 · Acquisition moments tied to economic pain

W1/W2 support moments such as:

- collection outgrows memory;
- spreadsheet/notes become cumbersome;
- photo history fragments;
- tracker fails;
- export/data concern;
- desire to see progression.

**Question:**  
Which of these moments can W4 turn into an acquisition loop where the user receives
differentiated value **before** migration/logging cost dominates?

## W4-Q3 · Migration as acquisition cost

Full-collection entry is possible, but its effort/abandonment rate is unvalidated.

**Question:**  
How should W4 model migration/import/setup as part of CAC/activation rather than as a
pure Product/UIUX problem?

## W4-Q4 · P2 as funnel versus revenue

**Question:**  
Does W4 find a plausible loop in which an acquisition/first-tree moment feeds a
surviving record/workflow job even if P2 itself has weak direct monetization?

W2 needs the distinction between:
- direct revenue;
- CAC reduction;
- activation;
- evidence generation;
- downstream retention.

## W4-Q5 · Comparable Journey loop

**Question:**  
Can Comparable Journey create a loop where:
documented journey → useful comparison → return/contribution → richer corpus,
without requiring a generic social feed or authority-dependent advice?

If W4 cannot identify a plausible loop, that weakens the economic upside of A3.

## W4-Q6 · CAC/payback discipline

**Question:**  
For any loop W4 recommends, what payer type ultimately funds it, and what must W2 know
before a maximum rational CAC/payback window can be calculated?

Do not optimize scale before payer economics are known.
