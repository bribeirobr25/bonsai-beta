## 0. Governing question

> **Which surviving opportunity areas have enough evidence, economic leverage and falsifiability to deserve scarce validation effort before Cross-Front Reconciliation?**

This ranking is about **validation priority**.

It is not a statement that the highest-ranked opportunity is the future product.

A high rank can mean:

- strong current evidence but large economic uncertainty;
- weak evidence but unusually high strategic/economic leverage;
- a hypothesis that cheaply resolves whether an entire economic branch is worth pursuing.

---

# 1. Ranking dimensions

W2 evaluates each opportunity area qualitatively across eight dimensions.

No numeric score is used because current evidence cannot justify false precision.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **Evidence of job/value** | Is the underlying behavior or pain actually observed? |
| **Value timing** | Does meaningful value arrive immediately, recurrently, or only after months/years? |
| **Frequency** | How often can value recur for the recipient? |
| **Substitute pressure** | How well do Excel, Photos, notes, forums, WhatsApp, course platforms or analog systems already solve it? |
| **Payer clarity** | Is there an identifiable actor with a reason and budget to pay? |
| **Contribution potential** | Could net contribution per payer/event plausibly support a meaningful business? |
| **Operational burden** | Does the model imply support, moderation, practitioner verification, AI, custody or sales burden? |
| **Dependency load** | How many unvalidated layers must work before the opportunity itself creates value? |

Priority labels:

- **A · Validate now** — high information value for W2/Cross-Front Reconciliation.
- **B · Validate after dependency** — potentially important, but another gate should pass first.
- **C · Supporting economic layer** — useful as retention/activation/value support, but weak as standalone economic thesis.
- **D · Defer / do not use to rescue economics** — blocked, contradicted as differentiation, or dependent on future authority.

# 2. Priority ranking

## A1 · Collection workflow: organization + retrieval + memory
### Validation priority: **HIGHEST**

**Related candidate substrate:** P1, but P1 is not selected.

### Evidence status

`OBSERVED`
- longitudinal recordkeeping exists;
- dedicated-app persistence exists;
- general-purpose and analog substitutes exist;
- collection growth can create organization/retrieval pressure;
- capture burden can also create exit;
- some users migrate or consider migrating whole collections;
- some experienced/large-collection users still prefer generic tools.

### Economic thesis

> A dedicated system creates current economic value only where workflow fragmentation, memory pressure and retrieval cost become worse than migration + capture + payment + continuity risk.

### Why A1 ranks first

1. It is the most behaviorally grounded opportunity area.
2. It can create value **before** years of progression exist.
3. It directly tests whether dedicated software deserves to exist alongside good-enough substitutes.
4. Failure materially weakens consumer P1 economics.
5. It can be tested without committing to product architecture or pricing.
6. It supplies the denominator for later consumer payment tests.

### Main risks

- high substitute pressure;
- users may value the job but not dedicated software;
- workflow pain may exist only in a small subset;
- sophisticated existing systems increase switching cost;
- data durability/continuity can raise trust requirements.

### Payer clarity

Consumer payer is plausible but `UNVALIDATED`.

### Economic ceiling

Unknown and highly dependent on G11 substitute filtering.

### Primary gates

`G1 Substitute superiority`  
`G2 Time to differentiated current value`  
then `G11 Economically eligible ceiling`.

### Kill condition

If pain-qualified growers across relevant workflow bands do not shift real behavior toward a dedicated workflow, stop treating consumer recordkeeping as a primary economic thesis.

---

## A2 · Teacher / practitioner workflow
### Validation priority: **VERY HIGH**

**Related candidate:** P3, still `UNVALIDATED`.

### Evidence status

`OBSERVED`
- organized teaching and practitioner relationships exist in bonsai;
- professional bonsai education carries monetary value;
- teacher/student collaboration features are already being attempted by competitors.

`UNVALIDATED`
- recurring teacher workflow pain;
- dedicated software advantage;
- teacher/practitioner SaaS WTP;
- budget ownership;
- reachable payer universe;
- sales/support economics.

### Economic thesis

> A professional actor may have a smaller but higher-value recurring workflow where the payer and beneficiary are clearer than in consumer tracking.

### Why A2 ranks this high despite weaker evidence

A2 has unusually high **economic information leverage**.

A small test can reveal whether:
- a professional payer surface exists at all;
- the venture has a higher-contribution path than low-price consumer tracking;
- P3 deserves to remain a candidate ceiling.

Failure is valuable because it removes the strongest unproven “higher ARPA” escape hatch.

### Main risks

- low number of reachable payers;
- existing tools may be good enough;
- long sales cycle;
- high onboarding/support/customization;
- teachers may value a student tool without controlling a budget;
- P3 may depend on P1 substrate for eventual product value.

### Payer clarity

Potential payer is identifiable, but budget ownership is `UNVALIDATED`.

### Economic ceiling

Potentially higher contribution per payer; total payer universe completely unknown.

### Primary gates

`G7A Workflow value`  
`G7B Budget ownership`  
`G7C Reachable payer ceiling`.

### Kill condition

If practitioners cannot demonstrate a recurring underserved workflow or qualified practitioners refuse real financial commitment, stop treating P3 as the project's presumed economic ceiling.

---

## A3 · Comparable Journey
### Validation priority: **HIGH**

**Related substrate:** may extend P1, but is not authority-dependent advice.

### Evidence status

`OBSERVED`
- progression threads function as learning infrastructure;
- growers seek and use longitudinal cases.

`UNVALIDATED`
- structured Comparable Journey product value;
- repeat use;
- contribution/payment impact;
- required record density.

### Economic thesis

> Comparable Journey may convert accumulated records from delayed passive history into recurrent current utility without requiring the system to tell the grower what to do.

### Why A3 ranks high

It attacks two of W2's hardest problems simultaneously:

1. delayed historical payoff;
2. authority barrier.

If successful, it can increase the frequency of value from a record substrate without requiring L4/L5 advice.

It is also cheap to test manually through curated matching before any algorithm or network exists.

### Main risks

- users may prefer Google/forum/direct expert answers;
- usefulness may require density/context the venture cannot obtain;
- product utility may not translate into payment;
- matching quality may create significant data-structure demands later.

### Payer clarity

Consumer payer is possible but currently unclear.

### Economic ceiling

Unknown. The key question is whether it materially changes retention or contribution relative to record-only.

### Primary gates

`G10 Comparable Journey utility`  
then `G13 Incremental contribution`.

### Kill condition

If qualified growers do not return to manually curated comparable cases on a second real need, do not use Comparable Journey as the argument for recurring consumer value.

# 3. Priority B · Validate after a dependency passes

## B1 · Acquisition / first-tree companion

**Related candidate:** P2.

### Evidence status

`SUPPORTED SIGNAL`
- first/second-tree users experience uncertainty;
- acquisition, gift, workshop and first-problem moments are behaviorally plausible acquisition moments.

`UNVALIDATED`
- willingness to pay;
- frequency per consumer;
- purchase-conversion benefit;
- vision/material-assessment capability;
- whether the moment creates a durable relationship.

### Economic thesis

> A high-intent purchase/first-tree moment may create immediate value and low-friction acquisition, even if the consumer's own payment frequency is low.

### Why B, not A

The economic moment may be powerful, but:
- it is episodic;
- parts of P2 depend on authority or unvalidated vision capability;
- it may be more valuable as **funnel/distribution** into another validated job than as standalone economics.

### Validate after

A clear non-authority job is specified, or a partner outcome is identified.

### Primary economic questions

- Does the moment change purchase confidence or downstream activation?
- Who pays: buyer, nursery, workshop or nobody?
- Does acquisition economics improve another surviving model?

### Kill condition

If the moment generates curiosity but no measurable current behavior or commercial outcome, treat P2 as a weak funnel, not a business.

---

## B2 · Partner-funded / partner-value model

### Evidence status

`OBSERVED`
- partner distribution is being attempted in the category.

`UNVALIDATED`
- partner ROI;
- partner payment;
- subsidized economics.

### Economic thesis

> A nursery, workshop or club may fund or subsidize access if the software improves an economic outcome for that organization.

### Dependency

First prove a recipient value proposition or business outcome.

A partner cannot rationally pay for a product that has not yet demonstrated value to the recipient or to the partner.

### Why B

High potential as payer displacement / CAC reduction, but current evidence supports **distribution attempt**, not partner WTP.

### Primary gates

`G8A Distribution`  
`G8B Partner payment`.

### Kill condition

If partners distribute only when Bonsai bears all cost and no partner metric improves, preserve them as W4 distribution actors and remove them from W2 revenue assumptions.

---

## B3 · Provenance / handoff / attestation economics

### Evidence status

`OBSERVED`
- provenance has cultural and market relevance;
- institutional provenance exists;
- transferred history already exists in competitors.

`UNVALIDATED`
- incremental trust from graded evidence;
- payer;
- payment event;
- event frequency;
- attestation economics.

### Economic thesis

> Transfer, sale, inheritance, exhibition or practitioner involvement may create a rarer but higher-value event where trusted history becomes economically relevant.

### Why B

Potential value per event may be high, but:
- frequency is likely much lower than routine record/retrieval;
- payer is unclear;
- plain transfer/history is already commoditized;
- verified provenance requires governance/attestation costs.

### Primary gate

`G9 Provenance economics`.

### Kill condition

If real handoff events show appreciation but no behavior or payer commitment, provenance remains a trust/retention layer rather than standalone monetization.

# 4. Priority C · Supporting economic layers, not standalone theses

## C1 · Progression / longitudinal history

### Evidence status

`OBSERVED` as value.

### Economic interpretation

History is **core cumulative value**, but on current evidence it has a timing problem:

> repeated capture cost now, larger payoff later.

Therefore W2 should not treat “people value progression” as sufficient evidence for a standalone paid proposition.

### Economic role

Potentially:
- retention;
- switching-cost reduction if portable;
- emotional/cumulative value;
- substrate for Comparable Journey;
- future provenance.

### What would upgrade it

Evidence that progression itself causes real renewal/payment rather than being merely appreciated after it exists.

---

## C2 · Care-task memory / reminders

### Evidence status

`OBSERVED`
- reminders create value for some users;
- reminders also create noise/friction for others.

### Economic interpretation

Useful current value, but heavily commoditized and contextual.

### Economic role

Likely:
- activation support;
- habit support;
- retention feature;
- current-utility bridge.

### Why it is not a primary economic opportunity

Calendar/reminder functionality is already widely available inside and outside bonsai software, and W1 explicitly shows experienced growers can find it useless.

Do not use reminders as the venture's economic wedge without new evidence.

---

## C3 · Data durability / export / ownership

### Evidence status

`OBSERVED` as trust concern.

### Economic interpretation

This may be a **condition for commitment** rather than a value people pay extra for.

It can:
- reduce adoption resistance;
- reduce lock-in fear;
- support long-horizon trust.

It also creates business-model obligations.

### Economic role

Architecture/commitment constraint, not currently a standalone revenue thesis.

Primary gate: `G4 Portability / ownership`.

# 7. What this ranking says about P1 / P2 / P3

It does **not** select among them.

## P1

P1 contains the highest-priority economic substrate **and** the strongest substitute threat.

Its economic future depends first on A1:

> Is there a dedicated workflow advantage large enough to create current value?

If yes, A3 Comparable Journey may increase recurring value later.

If no, historical value alone should not rescue P1.

## P2

P2 remains plausible mainly as:

- high-intent acquisition moment;
- potential funnel;
- possible partner-distribution object.

Its standalone economics remain weakly evidenced because recipient frequency and payer clarity are low.

## P3

P3 remains the potentially higher-contribution branch, but evidence is weakest where it matters economically:

- real workflow pain;
- actual budget;
- reachable payer ceiling.

Therefore P3 is **high validation priority**, not “best economics.”

This distinction supports `PROPOSED CANON AMENDMENT W2-001`.

# 8. W2 hypotheses to carry toward Cross-Front Reconciliation

Subject to later validation, the following economic hypotheses currently deserve to survive:

## EH1 · Dedicated-value hypothesis

> There may be an economically viable consumer segment where workflow fragmentation makes general tools insufficient enough to justify dedicated software.

Status: `UNVALIDATED`, grounded by `OBSERVED` job/substitute behavior.

## EH2 · Immediate-before-cumulative hypothesis

> A viable record proposition probably needs meaningful current utility before long-horizon history becomes valuable.

Status: `SUPPORTED SIGNAL`.

## EH3 · Comparable-value hypothesis

> Comparable Journey is currently the most strategically interesting non-authority mechanism for converting accumulated records into recurring current utility.

Status: `UNVALIDATED`.

This is a W2 prioritization statement, not a product conclusion.

## EH4 · Professional-payer hypothesis

> Teacher/practitioner workflow may support higher contribution per payer than standalone consumer tracking, but only if workflow pain, budget ownership and reachable payer count all pass independently.

Status: `UNVALIDATED`.

## EH5 · Partner-separation hypothesis

> Nursery/workshop/club actors may matter economically as distribution even when they do not pay.

Distribution attempts: `OBSERVED`.  
Partner payment: `UNVALIDATED`.

## EH6 · Provenance-event hypothesis

> Provenance is more plausibly an event/trust/retention economic layer than a high-frequency standalone subscription job unless real handoff payment behavior proves otherwise.

Status: `INFERENCE` from current evidence.

## EH7 · Monetization-neutrality hypothesis

> No current evidence justifies privileging subscription, one-time, lifetime, freemium or hybrid before value and service-duration economics are tested.

Status: W2 recommendation.

## EH8 · Ceiling-risk hypothesis

> Venture-scale viability is more uncertain than small-business viability because substitute filtering may leave a materially smaller economically eligible consumer segment than the hobby population.

Status: `SUPPORTED SIGNAL`; absolute market ceiling `UNVALIDATED`.

# 10. Stop-spending rules

W2 should stop adding research effort to an opportunity when:

### Collection workflow
Pain-present users do not shift real workflow under G1.

### Professional workflow
Artifact research fails G7A; do not run pricing tests for a job that does not exist.

### Comparable Journey
Manual curation fails repeat-use G10; do not build matching infrastructure.

### Acquisition companion
No measurable current behavior/commercial outcome appears; do not add vision/AI complexity.

### Partner model
Distribution works but partner ROI does not; transfer the finding to W4 and stop modeling partner revenue.

### Provenance
No real handoff payer/event emerges; keep provenance as trust substrate only.

### Decision Intelligence
Remain deferred unless authority gates independently pass.

This prevents “interesting idea” from becoming a permanent research budget category.

# 11. Implications for W3

W3 should not receive “the highest-ranked feature.”

It should receive the economic tensions:

1. **Current utility vs future meaning**  
   Users may pay for organization/retrieval while emotionally valuing progression/history.

2. **Ownership vs recurring revenue**  
   A brand promise of continuity can conflict with a monetization experience that feels like renting one's own record.

3. **Authority restraint**  
   Higher-value advisor language cannot be used to inflate perceived value before authority exists.

4. **Professional vs consumer meaning**  
   If professional economics survive, W3 may face multiple value recipients and payer narratives rather than one universal brand promise.

---

# 12. Implications for W4

W4 should receive:

1. A1 pain transitions as potential acquisition moments:
   - current system becomes cumbersome;
   - retrieval fails;
   - collection complexity rises;
   - tracker failure/export concern.

2. A partner can be valuable as **distribution actor without being payer**.

3. P2/acquisition should be evaluated partly on:
   - CAC reduction;
   - activation into a surviving job;
   - downstream retention,
   not merely direct revenue.

4. Growth loops that require large migration before current value are economically suspect.

5. W4 should not scale channels before W2 can state which acquired behavior has contribution value.

# 14. Ranking conclusion

The ranking produces three distinct economic frontiers.

## Frontier 1 · Prove dedicated consumer value

**Collection workflow / retrieval** is first because it determines whether a dedicated system can beat free/general substitutes at all.

## Frontier 2 · Test a different payer universe

**Teacher/practitioner workflow** is second because it can alter contribution and ceiling, not because its economics are already better.

## Frontier 3 · Create recurring value without authority

**Comparable Journey** is third because it could make the accumulated record useful repeatedly before any advisory claim exists.

Everything else currently depends on one of those frontiers, appears lower-frequency, or is better treated as a supporting layer.

> **Current W2 recommendation: spend validation effort on the hypotheses that can change the economic shape of the venture, not on monetization mechanics that merely change how an unproven value proposition is billed.**
