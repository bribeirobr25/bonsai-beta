## D · Four questions that would sharpen W2–W4

### D1 · W2 — when does perceived value arrive?

Your Explanation 3 is the sharpest diagnosis in the package: *the user pays the
logging cost every time they touch a tree, while some benefit arrives months or
years later.* That reframes the problem from friction to a **cost/benefit timing
mismatch**.

> **Does your evidence say anything about *when* users first report the record
> paying off — first week, first season, first year?** If value only arrives at
> the first repot or the first seasonal comparison, that dictates both the
> monetisation moment and the MVP.

### D2 · W2 — is one-time purchase the fit model?

Bonsai Album led the category for fifteen years on **buy-once**. Subscription
rejection is `OBSERVED`, and you raise whether subscription is structurally
mismatched with a decades-long continuity expectation.

> **Does any evidence bear on lifetime/one-time versus subscription beyond
> stated preference?** Renewal behaviour, migration after price change, anything
> revealed rather than declared.

### D3 · W3 — which language is theirs, not ours?

You list credible territories: memory, continuity, progression, calm
organisation, ownership of history.

> **Which of those phrases appear in users' own unprompted words, and which are
> our synthesis?** We would rather build brand on borrowed language than on
> our own vocabulary reflected back.

### D4 · W4 — did anyone successfully onboard a whole collection?

Your activation warning — *if activation requires importing 30 trees before
value becomes visible, switching cost kills the loop* — is a hard constraint on
any MVP.

> **Is there any observed case of a grower successfully migrating a full
> collection into a dedicated app, and what did they say about the effort?** A
> single positive case would tell us whether the barrier is absolute or merely
> high.
