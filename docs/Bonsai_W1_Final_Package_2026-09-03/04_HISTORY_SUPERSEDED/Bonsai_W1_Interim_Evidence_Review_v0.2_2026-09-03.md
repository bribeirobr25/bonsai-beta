# Bonsai · W1 · Interim Evidence Review v0.2

**Date:** 3 September 2026  
**Status:** PRE-VALIDATION / Track A  
**Corpus:** 43 derived, deduplicated review units  
**Purpose:** update the calibration evidence after competitor-master reconciliation and first cross-platform expansion.

## 1. What changed since v0.1

1. The known competitor universe was reconciled from **18 to at least 27 distinct products**.
2. Google Play review evidence was added for Bonsai Care App.
3. The Apple discovery pass confirmed that several 2026 entrants explicitly position around **low-friction logging**, offline/private records, photo timelines and climate/season-aware care.
4. The review-mining question is therefore sharper: not “has anyone tried logging?”, but **which implementations create durable value and which friction patterns still defeat them?**

## 2. Evidence update

### Tracking/history value
Still a **SUPPORTED SIGNAL**.

Across old and new reviews, users describe value in:
- collection management;
- replacing spreadsheets/camera-roll fragmentation;
- photo progression;
- keeping care/history in one place;
- remembering what happened to each tree.

### Paid value
Cross-platform evidence is now stronger but remains non-prevalence evidence.

Google Play includes a verified review from a user who says they moved immediately to Pro because the price felt worth it, while valuing collection management and care information. Another review values multiple collections in Pro.

Correct interpretation:
- **OBSERVED:** some users explicitly pay and report value.
- **UNVALIDATED:** paid conversion rate, retention distribution, ARPU, LTV, market viability.

### Logging friction
The strongest negative signals remain implementation-specific:
- photo-entry friction;
- retrieval/navigation;
- reminder semantics;
- login/localization blockers;
- data-loss/reliability;
- timeline/event-entry limitations.

There is still insufficient evidence to conclude that the underlying act of longitudinal logging is itself the dominant abandonment cause.

### Competitor response to friction
New 2026 entrants increasingly market:
- one-tap logging;
- “no forms”;
- offline/no-account use;
- no cloud;
- no nagging;
- fast reminders;
- direct notification actions;
- ZIP/PDF/CSV export.

This is **OBSERVED competitor positioning**, not user validation.

Strategic implication:
the market is already trying to solve logging friction explicitly. Therefore “we will make logging easier” cannot be treated as a differentiated thesis without behavioural evidence that a specific friction remains unresolved.

## 4. New contradiction pressure

No canonical product thesis is contradicted yet, but one assumption deserves pressure:

> “Superior low-friction logging may be a differentiated wedge.”

Current market evidence shows multiple new products already positioning directly on this claim.

Status:
**UNVALIDATED as a wedge.**

The existence of many attempts does not prove they solve it. W1 now needs to find whether users still report:
- capture friction,
- retrieval friction,
- photo friction,
- notification fatigue,
- trust/data durability problems,
- or eventual abandonment despite “low friction” claims.

**Expanding / large collection**

## 6. Review-source limitation

The v0.2 corpus is not a prevalence sample. Storefront algorithms choose what is displayed, newer apps often have too few reviews, and generic praise carries less behavioural information than specific use/failure reports.

For broad W1 analysis, each source must retain:
- platform;
- storefront;
- retrieval date;
- review date;
- product;
- whether review was developer-responded;
- historical/current-status classification;
- authenticity-risk flag where appropriate.

## 7. Current W1 judgment

The strongest provisional interpretation remains:

> **Users do show real value from tracking/history/progression, while the most observable complaints concern implementation, workflow and trust rather than wholesale rejection of the job.**

But the competitive landscape now raises the bar:
**many products already promise precisely that value with precisely those friction fixes.**

Therefore W1 must next determine not merely whether logging has value, but:
1. which value survives after novelty;
2. which friction repeatedly causes abandonment;
3. which jobs are still unresolved across products;
4. whether history/progression is enough to sustain recurring use without reminders or advice.

No MVP or product selection follows from this result.
