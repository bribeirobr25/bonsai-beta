## 0. Purpose

This document converts the W2 economic hypotheses into falsifiable validation gates.

It does **not** choose P1, P2 or P3.  
It does **not** choose subscription, one-time, freemium or pricing.  
It does **not** convert W1 counts into market prevalence.

The governing economic question remains:

> **Is there a plausible economic structure behind any surviving opportunity area once good-enough substitutes, repeated capture cost, delayed value, payment resistance and long-horizon continuity are included?**

---

## 1. Status discipline

Canonical statuses remain:

`OBSERVED` · `SUPPORTED SIGNAL` · `CONTRADICTED` · `UNVALIDATED` · `DEFERRED`.

The labels below — `STRONG`, `MIXED`, `FAIL` — are **W2 decision outcomes for a completed test**, not replacements for canonical evidence status.

A test that has not run is `UNVALIDATED`, never `FAIL`.

### Evidence hierarchy for W2 economic tests

Prefer, in descending order:

1. actual payment / renewal / contract / deposit;
2. actual switching or continued use when the current substitute remains available;
3. real workflow behavior with artifacts;
4. revealed choice with meaningful friction or opportunity cost;
5. stated willingness to pay;
6. general stated interest.

Stated interest alone does not pass an economic gate.

---

## 2. Threshold philosophy

W2 should not invent universal SaaS benchmarks for a niche category with no reliable retention or conversion baseline.

Therefore the matrix uses two kinds of threshold:

### A · Behavioral gate
A pre-registered minimum behavior in a deliberately recruited target cohort.

These thresholds answer:

> “Is there enough evidence to justify the next economic test?”

They are **not market-prevalence estimates**.

### B · Economic viability gate
Observed parameters are inserted into the economic ceiling model.

These thresholds answer:

> “Does this model support the selected ambition under conservative/base/upside conditions?”

Whenever an economic threshold depends on price, retention, CAC or market size that has not yet been measured, W2 defines the **decision rule now** rather than fabricating the missing number.

# 3. Success ambitions

The founder has not yet selected one exclusive venture ambition. W2 therefore keeps three ambitions in parallel.

## S1 · Sustainable small business

Economic success means:

- positive contribution after direct variable/service costs;
- enough annual contribution to sustain ongoing operations;
- no dependence on permanently subsidized free custody;
- acquisition and support can be funded without external capital indefinitely.

The exact founder-income target remains a founder decision.

## S2 · Founder full-time business

Economic success additionally means enough annual contribution to fund:

- founder compensation;
- product maintenance;
- support/operations;
- compliance/payment overhead;
- reinvestment and a reserve for churn/continuity.

## S3 · Larger / venture-scale opportunity

Economic success requires a credible path to a materially larger contribution pool without relying on:

- arbitrary expansion from bonsai into unrelated categories;
- unsupported TAM extrapolation;
- authority-dependent L4/L5 advice that has not passed its own validation gate;
- permanent negative unit economics masked by growth.

### Temporary W2 scenario anchors

The existing reverse-ceiling exercise uses **scenario anchors only**:

- €75k annual contribution · S1 planning case;
- €200k annual contribution · S2 planning case;
- €3m annual contribution · S3 order-of-magnitude stress test.

These are **not canon and not venture targets**. They exist only to expose payer-count sensitivity until the founder defines success more precisely.

# 5. G1 · Substitute-superiority gate
## E1 v0.2 · Workflow-complexity threshold

### Hypothesis

> A workflow-complexity threshold exists where the cost of fragmentation in general tools exceeds the combined switching, capture, payment and continuity cost of dedicated software.

### Source basis

W1 `OBSERVED`:
- Excel, spreadsheets, photo folders, generic notes, Lightroom, paper and reminders are real substitute workflows;
- dedicated-app persistence and dedicated-app exit both exist;
- collection scale increases both organization value and capture/retrieval burden.

### Test population

Recruit by **workflow complexity**, not merely by experience:

- 1–5 trees;
- 6–20;
- 21–50;
- 51+;

while separately recording:

- number of tools used;
- intervention frequency;
- photo volume;
- retrieval frequency;
- current-system satisfaction;
- existing switching cost.

The bands are sampling scaffolding, not predicted thresholds.

### Required test

Use a minimal research instrument capable of solving the record/retrieval job without requiring a product build.

Participants continue to have access to their existing system.

Measure:

1. real records captured;
2. real retrieval tasks completed;
3. real migration behavior;
4. unprompted continuation;
5. whether the participant moves part of the real workflow to the research instrument.

### Proposed behavioral threshold

For a **pre-qualified pain-present segment**:

**STRONG**
- ≥50% continue using the dedicated workflow without researcher prompting through the observation window; **and**
- ≥30% move a meaningful part of their real record workflow away from, or structurally integrate it beyond, their existing substitute.

**MIXED**
- 25–49% continue unprompted; or
- 15–29% make a meaningful workflow shift.

**FAIL**
- <25% continue unprompted **or**
- <15% make a meaningful workflow shift,
despite the cohort having demonstrated pre-existing workflow pain.

### Interpretation warning

This is a targeted-cohort decision threshold, not market prevalence.

A strong G1 still requires G11 to determine whether the segment is large enough.

### Failure scope

A G1 fail in one workflow band kills that **segment proposition**, not the whole venture.

If G1 fails across beginner, expanding and experienced consumer cohorts, consumer P1 economics are severely weakened.

# 6. G2 · Time-to-differentiated-value gate
## E2 · Immediate utility versus delayed history

### Hypothesis

> Immediate utility arrives early enough that the user receives differentiated value before progression/history has had months or years to accumulate.

### Source basis

W1 distinguishes:

**Immediate value**
- cataloging;
- remembering;
- centralizing;
- replacing a cumbersome system.

**Delayed value**
- progression comparison;
- later retrieval;
- seasonal comparison;
- future decision context.

W1 cannot establish the delay.

### Primary endpoint

A **meaningful differentiated value event** before long-term progression exists.

It must be behavioral, for example:

- retrieving a real prior intervention faster/more reliably than the current workflow;
- completing a real collection-level task the current workflow makes difficult;
- returning voluntarily because the instrument currently helps, not because future history sounds appealing.

### Proposed behavioral threshold

Within a qualified cohort:

**STRONG**
- ≥50% experience at least one meaningful differentiated value event within 7 days; **and**
- ≥40% voluntarily return for current utility before any long-term progression payoff exists.

**MIXED**
- 25–49% experience the event or
- 20–39% voluntarily return.

**FAIL**
- <25% experience differentiated current value, or value is mainly described as “this will be useful someday.”

### Failure scope

Kills a pure delayed-history consumer proposition unless another payer/value mechanism independently passes.

Does not kill provenance, professional workflow or acquisition-moment models by itself.

# 7. G3 · Recurring-value / recurring-payment gate
## E3 · Consumer recurring economics

### Hypothesis

> At least one consumer segment receives recurring **current** value sufficient to support recurring payment.

### Important distinction

Subscription rejection is `OBSERVED`, but paid multi-year use is also `OBSERVED`.

Therefore W2 must not use “likes subscription” as the endpoint.

### Required evidence

A real-money paid test with:

- real cancellation opportunity;
- renewal or continuation decision;
- current substitute still available;
- direct variable/service costs measured;
- reason for continued payment coded.

### Primary economic decision rule

Do **not** set a universal retention threshold before price and contribution are observed.

For each tested paid model calculate:

`observed retained payers × net annual contribution per payer`

and the implied number of active paying units required for S1/S2/S3.

### STRONG

- real paid continuation is observed;
- continued payment is attributed to current/recurring value rather than fear of losing historical data;
- conservative contribution parameters produce a payer requirement that G11 can plausibly support.

### MIXED

- paid continuation exists, but only the base/upside economic case reaches the ambition; or
- continuation appears partly lock-in/custody-driven rather than current-value-driven.

### FAIL

- paid continuation disappears when real renewal is required; **or**
- even the credible upside contribution/retention case requires more eligible payers than G11 can support.

### Failure scope

Kills recurring consumer monetization.

Does **not** automatically kill one-time, transaction, professional or partner-funded structures.

# 8. G4 · Portability / ownership gate
## E4 · Export and data ownership

### Hypothesis

> Data portability materially reduces commitment resistance and improves economically relevant adoption/retention.

### Source basis

W1 `OBSERVED`:
- fear of product disappearance;
- concern about export before committing a collection;
- reaction to apparent data loss;
- preference for durable systems.

### Required test

Randomized or matched comparison where the economic proposition is identical except for a clearly defined portability/continuity guarantee.

Primary endpoint should be:

- real activation/migration;
- paid commitment;
- or renewal,

not “I like export.”

### Decision threshold

Numeric effect-size threshold should be pre-registered **after baseline conversion is known**.

Current W2 rule:

**STRONG**
Portability produces a material lift in real commitment large enough to exceed the direct cost/complexity it adds.

**MIXED**
Trust preference is strong but behavior lift is small or uncertain.

**FAIL**
Portability changes stated preference but not real commitment.

### Failure scope

A fail means portability may remain a trust requirement without being a monetization driver.

It does not justify removing export/ownership if another workstream establishes it as a trust requirement.

# 9. G5 · Non-subscription / hybrid economics gate
## E5

### Hypothesis

> A one-time, lifetime, transaction or hybrid structure reduces business-model rejection without making the long-term service obligation uneconomic.

### Required evidence

Compare candidate economic structures using:

- real-money choice;
- direct service/custody cost;
- expected support;
- payment fees;
- duration of revenue;
- duration of data/service obligation.

### Primary decision rule

Evaluate at a common long-horizon window, for example 5 and 10 years:

`cumulative net contribution per acquired payer`

versus

`cumulative direct + allocated long-horizon obligation`.

### STRONG

A non-recurring/hybrid model:
- materially improves real commitment versus recurring-only; **and**
- remains contribution-positive under conservative heavy-user and inactive-custody scenarios.

### MIXED

Choice improves but economics require limits, separate recurring services, or a narrower custody promise.

### FAIL

The model attracts users only by accepting a service obligation that becomes structurally negative once revenue stops.

### Failure scope

Kills that monetization architecture, not the opportunity.

# 10. G6 · Experienced-grower segment gate
## E6

### Hypothesis

> Experienced growers' higher record value compensates for smaller segment size, stronger existing workflows and higher customization/switching demands.

### Source basis

W1 shows both:
- experienced/large-collection value in history and organization;
- experienced rejection of generic reminders/subscription and preference for custom workflows.

### Required evidence

Measure separately:

- current workaround sophistication;
- switching/migration time;
- required customization;
- support burden;
- actual payment;
- annual contribution;
- reachable segment size.

### Economic decision rule

**STRONG**
Conservative segment contribution exceeds the selected ambition threshold with a margin of safety after support/acquisition costs.

**MIXED**
Base case works but conservative case does not.

**FAIL**
Even optimistic credible contribution × reachable payer count cannot meet S1, or customization/support destroys contribution.

### Failure scope

Kills experienced-grower-first economics, not beginner/expanding/professional models.

# 11. G7 · Professional economics
## E7A · Workflow value

### Hypothesis

Teachers/practitioners have a recurring workflow job materially underserved by WhatsApp, photos, Drive, spreadsheets, course platforms or existing tools.

### Required evidence

Artifact/workflow research first.

Count a job only when the practitioner demonstrates a recurring current workflow, not when they react positively to a feature concept.

### Proposed threshold

In a deliberately recruited professional cohort:

**STRONG**
- ≥50% demonstrate a repeated workflow with measurable time/coordination cost; and
- ≥30% voluntarily use a concierge/manual instrument for real work through at least one real cycle.

**MIXED**
- 25–49% demonstrate the repeated job or
- 15–29% adopt the instrument.

**FAIL**
The job is rare, episodic, or existing tools are judged sufficient.

---

## E7B · Budget ownership

### Hypothesis

The actor receiving the professional value controls or can access a real budget for it.

### Evidence hierarchy

1. paid invoice / contract;
2. deposit / budget allocation;
3. written procurement commitment;
4. stated WTP.

### Proposed threshold

**STRONG**
At least 30% of the **qualified job-positive cohort** make a real non-zero financial commitment to continue the workflow.

**MIXED**
10–29% commit, or budget exists but requires organizational approval/sales friction.

**FAIL**
<10% make a real commitment, or the beneficiary has no budget authority.

This is a targeted-cohort gate, not market conversion.

---

## E7C · Reachable payer ceiling

### Hypothesis

There are enough economically reachable professional payers after sales/support friction.

### Decision rule

Use:

`reachable payer universe × realistic penetration × net annual contribution`

and compare with S1/S2/S3.

**STRONG**
Conservative case ≥2× the payer/contribution requirement for the selected ambition.

**MIXED**
Base case reaches the ambition but conservative case does not.

**FAIL**
Even credible upside does not reach the ambition.

### Failure scope

Failure of E7A/B/C kills P3 as a primary economic thesis.

P3 may still remain strategically useful as an attestation/authority layer if another payer funds it.

### Hypothesis

Nursery/workshop/club receives a measurable commercial outcome sufficient to fund or subsidize access.

### Required outcome

At least one measurable business metric must move:

- purchase conversion;
- staff service time;
- repeat purchase;
- workshop revenue;
- complaint/return reduction;

**FAIL**

# 13. G9 · Provenance economics
## E9

### Hypothesis

Provenance produces monetizable value at transfer, sale, inheritance, exhibition or practitioner-involvement moments.

### Current evidence

Cultural/market relevance of provenance is `OBSERVED`; willingness to pay for a provenance service is `UNVALIDATED`.

### Test

Use real handoff/transfer situations where possible.

Measure:
- whether evidence is actually assembled;
- who values it;
- who would pay;
- whether payment is transaction-based, service-based or bundled;
- frequency of the economic event.

### Decision rule

**STRONG**
A repeatable payer/event exists and conservative annual event volume × net contribution supports at least S1 or materially improves another surviving model.

**MIXED**
High value per event but event frequency/market size is too low to stand alone.

**FAIL**
Provenance is appreciated but produces no payer behavior or economically meaningful event.

### Failure scope

Usually kills provenance as standalone monetization, not provenance as retention/trust value.

# 14. G10 · Comparable Journey economics
## E10

### Hypothesis

Comparable Journey creates recurring utility before advisory authority exists and can increase retention/contribution.

### Current evidence

Progression threads as learning infrastructure are `OBSERVED`.

Product value of structured Comparable Journey is `UNVALIDATED`.

W3 and W4 support keeping Comparable Journey in a **Learning** frame, not prematurely treating it as AI advice, community or evidence-based recommendation.

---

## G10-A · Corpus-density feasibility
### Cross-front mapping: W4 L4-A

Before participant utility testing, test whether realistic comparable cases can exist at sufficient density.

Relevant context dimensions can include:

- species;
- climate;
- development stage;
- intervention;
- pre-intervention state;
- relevant outcome window.

The purpose is not to build an algorithm.

It is to determine whether contextual filtering leaves enough real records to make comparison meaningful.

### Required test

Construct a realistic shadow corpus from available evidence/data sources and predefine a set of priority target cases.

For each target case record:

- whether a comparable case exists;
- how many independent comparables exist;
- which contextual constraints had to be relaxed;
- whether relaxing those constraints makes the comparison generic or misleading;
- whether density is concentrated in only a few species/stages.

### Decision rule

A universal numeric density threshold is **not yet justified** because the target context set has not been defined.

The exact coverage threshold must be pre-registered before execution.

**STRONG**
- useful matches exist across most priority target cases;
- multiple independent comparable records are available in the important contexts;
- relevance does not depend on dropping load-bearing context constraints.

**MIXED**
- useful density exists only in a narrow subset of species/stages;
- or matches require relaxing one meaningful context dimension.

**FAIL**
- realistic priority cases frequently have no useful comparable record;
- or relevance only appears after collapsing context so far that the result becomes generic.

### Failure scope

A G10-A fail kills Comparable Journey as a near-term differentiated value mechanism under the available corpus.

It does **not** kill:
- longitudinal recordkeeping;
- progression/history value;
- future re-testing if materially richer data later exists.

---

## G10-B · Utility / repeat behavior
### Cross-front mapping: W4 L4-B

Run only if G10-A survives.

Use curated/manual matching before any recommendation system.

Measure:

- selection over generic search/forum;
- repeat use;
- effect on return;
- effect on willingness to maintain structured records.

Payment impact is evaluated **only after** utility/repeat behavior exists.

### Proposed behavioral threshold

**STRONG**
- ≥40% of qualified cases voluntarily use a comparable journey when offered instead of generic discovery; and
- ≥30% return to the mechanic on a second real decision/problem without prompting.

**MIXED**
- 20–39% first use; or
- 15–29% repeat.

**FAIL**
Users prefer direct answers, existing search/forum, or do not return.

### Economic follow-on

Only after G10-B survives should W2 test:

1. retention / repeat-use effect;
2. contribution effect;
3. WTP / packaging effect.

Do not infer monetization from G10-A or G10-B alone.

### Failure scope

Kills Comparable Journey as the mechanism that converts accumulated records into near-term recurring value.

Does not kill the underlying record job.

# 15. G11 · Economically eligible market ceiling
## E11

### Hypothesis

After removing users satisfied by general/analog substitutes, the remaining economically eligible population is large enough for the intended ambition.

### Required segmentation

Do **not** start from total bonsai audience.

Estimate separately:

1. job exists;
2. software willingness;
3. current substitute adequacy;
4. dedicated advantage;
5. activation/migration;
6. payer behavior;
7. retained payer behavior.

### Decision rule

For each ambition and payer model:

**STRONG**
The **conservative** eligible-payer case supports ≥2× the active-payer requirement.

**MIXED**
The base case supports the requirement but conservative case does not, or conservative case supports only 1–2×.

**FAIL**
Even the credible upside case produces fewer eligible payers/contribution than required.

### Why 2×

The 2× margin is a **W2 proposed uncertainty buffer**, not a market fact. It prevents a highly uncertain niche market model from being greenlit when its entire theoretical population is barely equal to the required paying base.

### Failure scope

- Failure for S3 does **not** kill S1/S2.
- Failure for S2 does **not** kill S1.
- Failure even for S1 is a venture-level warning.

# 16. G12 · Long-horizon funding gate
## E12

### Hypothesis

Long-term record custody can be funded sustainably under a trust-compatible business model.

### Required scenarios

At minimum model:

- light user;
- median/base user;
- heavy photo user;
- inactive former payer whose data persists;
- provider migration/export event;
- support/recovery event.

### Decision rule

**STRONG**
The model remains contribution-positive under a conservative 10-year obligation scenario **without** assuming perpetual payment from inactive users and while honoring the stated portability/continuity promise.

**MIXED**
Economics work only with explicit storage/service limits, separate recurring services, or a narrower custody promise.

**FAIL**
The proposition implicitly promises long-lived hosted history that cannot be funded once payment ends.

### Failure scope

Kills that custody/business-model architecture.

It does not kill the record opportunity if a different trust-compatible architecture can fund the promise.

# 17. G13 · Record-only contribution ceiling
## E13

### Hypothesis

Materially higher ARPU/contribution requires value beyond basic recordkeeping.

### Current status

`SUPPORTED SIGNAL`, not proven.

### Test

Once basic record value is established, compare real-money choices between:

- record/history only;
- record + high-frequency utility;
- record + collaboration;
- record + Comparable Journey;
- other independently validated value layers.

Do not bundle unvalidated authority/advice simply to manufacture a higher price.

### Decision rule

**STRONG**
Record-only contribution ceiling is materially lower, while an independently validated additional value layer raises net contribution enough to change the ambition supported.

**MIXED**
Additional value raises WTP but also raises cost enough that contribution barely changes.

**FAIL**
Users do not pay materially more for the additional validated value.

### Failure scope

Determines economic architecture, not product selection.

# 18. G14 · Price-sensitivity gate
## E14

### Hypothesis

Standalone tracking is materially price-sensitive.

### Current evidence

`SUPPORTED SIGNAL` from:
- subscription rejection in W1;
- provider pricing/discount behavior observed in W2.

No elasticity is known.

### Required test

Use real-money randomized price/offer tests **after** the value proposition under test is fixed.

Van Westendorp or stated-price surveys may be secondary only.

### Decision rule

Estimate:

- conversion elasticity;
- contribution per visitor/qualified user;
- renewal by price cohort;
- support/cost differences if packaging changes.

**STRONG**
Price materially changes real commitment and there is a contribution-maximizing region distinct from the highest-conversion price.

**MIXED**
Direction exists but confidence/volume is insufficient.

**FAIL**
No meaningful price response within the tested range.

### Important

This gate informs economics. It does not choose the final price.

# 19. Venture-level kill architecture

The current venture should not be killed because one monetization model fails.

Use three levels.

## Level A · Model kill

Examples:

- G3 fail → kill recurring consumer subscription thesis.
- G5 fail → kill tested one-time/hybrid structure.
- G8B fail → kill partner-as-payer, preserve partner-as-distribution if G8A passes.
- G9 fail → kill provenance as standalone monetization.
- G12 fail → kill that custody model.

## Level B · Candidate/segment kill

Examples:

- G1 + G2 fail across consumer workflow bands → P1 consumer economics severely weakened.
- G7A/B/C fail → P3 as primary economic model fails.
- G10 fail → Comparable Journey cannot currently carry recurring value.

## Level C · Venture-level kill / major rethink

A venture-level economic kill should require one of the following:

### VK1 · No incremental economic value
G1 fails across all material consumer segments **and** G7/E8 identify no professional/organizational payer with real economics.

### VK2 · No plausible payer
Real behavior shows value, but G3/G5/G7B/G8B/G9 collectively produce no payer mechanism that can fund the service.

### VK3 · Ceiling below even the smallest ambition
G11 credible upside cannot support S1 after substitute filtering and contribution costs.

### VK4 · Unfundable continuity promise
Every trust-compatible model that preserves the core long-horizon value fails G12.

### VK5 · Economics require unvalidated future authority
The venture becomes viable only by assuming revenue/value from Decision Intelligence, practitioner authority or other future layers that have not passed their own behavioral/authority gates.

### VK6 · CAC/support consumes the niche
Once actual acquisition, onboarding and support are observed, the attainable contribution pool disappears even under the credible upside market case.

A negative outcome on any VK condition is a successful W2 result: it prevents product construction on economically impossible assumptions.

# 21. Research sequence implied by economics

This matrix does **not** override the canonical cross-front sequence. It identifies what economic evidence a later MVP Validation Strategy must obtain.

## Before participant-access tests

W2 can complete:

- economic model census;
- cost envelopes;
- partner/professional universe research;
- competitor monetization patterns;
- reverse ceilings;
- market-size methodology;
- kill logic.

## Participant validation / economic evidence

Prioritize:

1. **G1 + G2** · substitute threshold and time-to-current-value;
2. **G7A + G7B** · professional workflow and actual budget;
3. **G11** · segment prevalence/eligible-ceiling inputs;
4. **G3/G5/G14** · real payment structure tests;
5. **G8B** · partner economic outcome;
6. **G10** · Comparable Journey economics;
7. **G9** · provenance monetization where real handoff opportunities exist;
8. **G12** · continuously update with observed storage/support/payment duration.

Why this order:

> There is little value in optimizing pricing before proving dedicated incremental value, and little value in estimating a professional ARPA before proving a professional job and budget.
